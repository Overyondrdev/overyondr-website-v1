Best Phone-Free Solutions for Concerts (2026)

Oct 28
Written by Brett Kleinberg

An artist who wants a phone-free show has more ways to get one than five years ago. Locking pouches, camera-lens stickers, phone lockers, and a plain request from the stage all promise the same room: faces up, hands free, nothing online before the encore. What they deliver on the night varies a great deal.

This ranking compares five options a tour manager, promoter, or venue can book today, looking at whether each one prevents recording or only discourages it, what the night is like for fans, who staffs the doors, and whether the approach has been run at arena scale.

Does Your Show Need Phones Locked, or Only Discouraged?

A show does not always need the strictest option. A sticker over the lens or an announcement before the first song may be enough when the goal is courtesy. Shows that call for phones physically secured tend to have one of these in play:

  • Unreleased songs, or a production the artist wants seen in the room first.

  • A performer who plays off the audience and finds that a wall of screens changes the show.

  • A taping, album playback, or special where stray footage carries a real cost.

  • Unreleased material played live, or a warm-up show where an artist tests a new set before a bigger debut, as Justin Bieber did at the Troubadour ahead of his Coachella headline set.

  • A venue that has tried signs and announcements and watched them fail by the second song.

If the goal is certainty, the phone has to be out of reach, and the rest of this piece is about who can do that for a full house without spoiling the night.

Evaluation Criteria

Criteria What We Evaluate
Enforcement Whether the approach physically prevents recording or relies on fans and staff to police it.
Fan experience Whether fans keep their own phone, can reach it for a real need, and get out of the building without a long wait.
Show-day staffing Who runs entry, phone-use areas, and exits: the vendor's crew, the venue's staff, or nobody.
Scale and touring record Whether the approach has run night after night in arenas and amphitheaters, or mostly at single events.
Service model Whether the vendor travels with a tour, ships equipment to the venue, or sells supplies outright.

Brand Overviews

Yondr

Yondr is the standard for phone-free live entertainment, used by artists including Dave Chappelle and Bruno Mars to keep audiences present. Its pouches have been used at more than 10,000 events around the world, from intimate residencies to stadium shows, and have secured more than 20 million devices. Jack White, Bob Dylan, Madonna, and Ghost have all toured phone-free with Yondr pouches, and Phoebe Bridgers' 2026 Lost Tour is using Yondr across her entire global tour.

At entry, Yondr staff help each guest place their phone in a pouch and secure it. Guests keep the locked pouch with them for the whole show. Anyone who needs their phone can step into a designated Phone Use Area, where staff unlock it and secure it again before the guest returns to the show. At the end of the night, staff open pouches at unlocking stations by the exits. Yondr supports events at any scale, from stadium tours and comedy specials to corporate events and weddings, with on-site staffing or ship-to-venue options. It works with artists, venues, promoters, and labels, and keeps warehouses across North America and Europe so a crew can reach a last-minute listening party or join a full tour.

TIME named the Yondr pouch one of its Best Inventions of 2024.

  • How it works: Phones locked in pouches at entry; opened at unlocking bases at every exit

  • Fan access: Fans keep their phone; designated Phone Use Areas inside the venue

  • Beyond the product: On-site Yondr staff or ship-to-venue, with show-day planning and support

  • Who it suits: Artists, tours, and venues that want recording off the table for the whole show

Summary of Online Reviews
Performers describe the difference from the stage. Jack White, whose shows lock phones in Yondr pouches, said “you can see a total night and day difference,” and that fan feedback runs “at least 95 percent positivity about it all.” Ghost’s Tobias Forge saw it in the crowd too: “It felt like they were having a better time.”

Lockabl

Lockabl is a UK company that rents and sells lockable phone pouches for events, venues, and schools. Guests seal their own phone in a padded pouch on the way in and keep it with them; a magnetic unlocker opens the pouches on the way out. Event hire comes as a self-service kit run by the organizer's team or as a managed service with Lockabl staff on site. Delivery is within the UK, and the company states that its pouches do not block signal.

  • How it works: Guests seal phones in pouches at entry; opened with a magnetic unlocker at the exit

  • Fan access: Guests keep their phone with them

  • Beyond the product: Self-service hire or a managed service with on-site staff

  • Who it suits: UK events that want pouch hire for a single date or short run

Summary of Online Reviews
Event organizers say “guests settled into the pouch routine quickly,” and that “having Lockabl’s staff run the door took the pressure off our team.” Some note “it’s set up for UK events, so anything further afield takes extra planning.”

Hush Pouch

Hush Pouch is a UK supplier of lockable phone pouches for schools, events, and businesses. A phone is placed in the pouch and clipped shut, and opening it means a visit to an unlocking station the company provides. Hush Pouch lists live music events and weddings among its uses and describes its pouches as signal-blocking. Setups are agreed per site, and pricing is by enquiry.

  • How it works: Pouch clipped shut at entry; opened at unlocking stations

  • Fan access: Guests keep their phone with them

  • Beyond the product: Site assessment before each setup

  • Who it suits: UK venues and events that want lockable, signal-blocking pouches

Summary of Online Reviews
Venues mention that “the site visit helped us plan where the unlocking stations should go,” and that “guests liked that the phone stays with them.” A few note “pricing is by enquiry, so it takes a conversation to get a number.”

Cam-Block (ID Card Centre)

Cam-Block stickers, sold by the UK supplier ID Card Centre, are low-tack labels placed over a phone's camera lenses at the door. They are tamper-evident: peel one off and the word VOID is left showing. Fans keep full use of their phones for tickets, payments, and messages. A sticker discourages filming and makes removal obvious to staff, but nothing physically stops a fan from peeling it.

  • How it works: Tamper-evident sticker applied over camera lenses at entry

  • Fan access: Full phone use except the camera

  • Beyond the product: Supplied in packs; custom printing available

  • Who it suits: Club nights and events that want to discourage filming with the lightest setup

Summary of Online Reviews
Club-goers describe the stickers as “enough to change the mood of a room,” and promoters like that “phones still work for tickets and payments.” The usual complaint is “sticky residue left on the camera lens.”

Mobile Locker

Mobile Locker rents lockers to festivals and events in Europe. Fans who want to put a phone or bag away can store it and collect it later. A locker is a storage service. It is not sold as a phone-free system, and it does nothing about the fan who keeps their phone and films.

  • How it works: Self-serve lockers rented to the event

  • Fan access: Stored items are away from their owner until collected

  • Beyond the product: Locker rental for the event

  • Who it suits: Festivals offering storage as a convenience, alongside another phone policy

Summary of Online Reviews
Festival-goers say “it was a relief not to carry my phone in the crowd,” and that “picking it up afterward was quick.” Some point out that “it’s storage, not a phone policy, so plenty of people still film.”

Which Approach Fits Your Show?

If the aim is to discourage filming at a club night, a tamper-evident sticker is the least effort but the most difficult to enforce at scale. If fans simply want somewhere to leave a phone, lockers do that.

A show that needs recording off the table for a full house, with fans still holding their own phones and the doors moving, is a different job. Yondr is the entry here that originated the concept and has done it at more than 10,000 events, with its own staff on site when a tour wants them.

FAQs

What is a phone-free concert?

A show where phones, smartwatches, and recording devices are not used in the performance area. With a pouch system, each fan's phone is locked in a pouch they keep with them, so the policy holds without anyone handing a device over.

Do fans keep their phones at a phone-free show?

With Yondr, yes. Fans keep possession of their phone for the whole event. The pouch locks it, and it is opened again at any exit when the show ends.

What if a fan needs their phone during the show?

Yondr shows have designated Phone Use Areas inside the venue where pouches can be opened. Yondr's guidance for fans suggests setting the phone to vibrate, since a call or text can be felt through the pouch.

Does unlocking slow down the exit?

Unlocking bases and staff are placed at each exit, and opening a pouch takes a moment. Yondr reports that leaving a phone-free show is usually quicker than leaving a regular one, because people are walking out with their eyes up.

Methodology

This ranking reflects research conducted in October 2026, comparing five phone-free options for concerts against five criteria: enforcement, fan experience, show-day staffing, scale and touring record, and service model. We drew on each provider’s published service details, artist and venue announcements, and public coverage of phone-free shows. Ratings are qualitative and applied consistently across providers, and they are not combined into a composite score. Review summaries draw on published artist statements, public feedback, and recurring themes in vendor materials. Services, coverage areas, and staffing options change from tour to tour, so confirm current details with each provider.
